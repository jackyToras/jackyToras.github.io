  <p>
    Completed an on-campus research internship (1st June – 15th July 2026) in the Department of Computer Engineering under the supervision of Dr. Amandeep Kaur, Assistant Professor. Worked on "Machine Learning and Deep Learning-Based Detection of Aphids and Whiteflies in Potato Crops: A Systematic Literature Review." Designed and executed a multi-database search strategy spanning Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, Springer, ACM Digital Library, Scopus, and ScienceDirect, combining pest-identification, detection-methodology, and machine-learning keyword groups with Boolean operators and wildcard truncation. Ran the review through a seven-stage screening pipeline — deduplication, title screening, abstract/full-text screening, and iterative reference snowballing — narrowing an initial pool of 973 records down to 43 primary studies. Built a structured classification framework across seven dimensions (algorithm category, computational weight, crop focus, dataset type, dataset conditions, study context, and empirical method) to enable cross-tabulated analysis of research trends. Categorized and compared algorithm families — CNNs, transfer learning, YOLO-family real-time detectors, ANN/traditional ML, hybrid/ensemble methods, and Vision Transformers — evaluating each on accuracy, inference speed, and field-deployment suitability. Identified and documented critical, actionable research gaps (including the complete absence of YOLO validation on potato-specific datasets and the near-total lack of Vision Transformer adoption), and compiled findings into a full manuscript with supporting figures, comparison tables, and a properly cited primary-studies reference list.
